## 6. 🟠 Energy Dissipation (Detailed & Visual)

### 🧩 Problem
A tennis ball is dropped from a height of:

- $h_0 = 2.0 \, \text{m}$

After each bounce:
- It **loses 30% of its mechanical energy**

👉 Question: To what height does it rise after the **second bounce**?

---

## 🔑 Key Idea

Mechanical energy is proportional to height:

$$
E \propto h
$$

👉 This means:
- If energy decreases, height decreases by the **same ratio**

---

## 📉 Energy Loss

- Energy lost each bounce: **30%**
- Energy retained: **70%**

$$
\text{Remaining energy ratio} = 0.70
$$

---

## 🖼️ Step-by-Step Visualization

### 🔽 Initial Drop

- Initial height:
  
$$
h_0 = 2.0 \, \text{m}
$$

---

### 🔼 After First Bounce

The ball keeps **70%** of its energy:

$$
h_1 = 2.0 \times 0.70
$$

$$
h_1 = 1.4 \, \text{m}
$$

---

### 🔼 After Second Bounce

Again, only **70%** is retained:

$$
h_2 = 1.4 \times 0.70
$$

$$
h_2 = 0.98 \, \text{m}
$$

---

## 🎯 Final Answer

$$
h_2 = \mathbf{0.98 \, \text{m}}
$$

---

## 🧠 Intuition

Each bounce:
- The ball loses energy due to **heat, sound, and deformation**
- So it **never reaches the original height**

---

## 📊 Pattern Insight

This is a **geometric decay**:

$$
h_n = h_0 \cdot (0.70)^n
$$

👉 For $n = 2$:

$$
h_2 = 2.0 \cdot (0.70)^2 = 0.98 \, \text{m}
$$

---

## 📌 Summary

- Energy ∝ height  
- Each bounce keeps **70%** of energy  
- Heights form a decreasing sequence  

👉 **Energy loss → lower bounce height each time**
