## 3. ⚡ Conservation of Energy (Detailed & Visual)

### 🧩 Problem
- Pendulum length: $L = 1.0 \, \text{m}$
- Initial angle: $\theta = 15^\circ$

👉 Question: What is the speed of the pendulum bob at the lowest point?

---

## 🔑 Key Idea

We use **conservation of energy**:

$$
\text{Potential Energy} = \text{Kinetic Energy}
$$

$$
mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

👉 The mass ($m$) cancels out, so we don’t need it.

---

## 🖼️ Physical Interpretation

- At the start:
  - The pendulum is **higher up** → energy is **potential**
  - Velocity = 0

- At the bottom:
  - Height = 0
  - Energy is entirely **kinetic**

👉 So: **potential energy → kinetic energy**

---

## 📏 Step 1: Find the Height

The vertical drop of the pendulum is:

$$
h = L(1 - \cos\theta)
$$

Substitute values:

$$
h = 1.0 \cdot (1 - \cos 15^\circ)
$$

$$
\cos 15^\circ \approx 0.966
$$

$$
h \approx 1.0 \cdot (1 - 0.966) = 0.0341 \, \text{m}
$$

---

## ⚡ Step 2: Calculate the Speed

From energy conservation:

$$
v = \sqrt{2gh}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.0341}
$$

$$
v \approx \sqrt{0.669} \approx 0.818 \, \text{m/s}
$$

---

## 🎯 Final Answer

$$
v \approx \mathbf{0.82 \, \text{m/s}}
$$

---

## 🧠 Quick Insight

- Small angle → small height → small speed  
- Larger angle → more energy → higher speed  

---

## 📌 Summary

- Height: $h = L(1 - \cos\theta)$  
- Energy: $mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$  
- Result: $v = \sqrt{2gh}$  

👉 **Energy is not lost — it is transformed.**
