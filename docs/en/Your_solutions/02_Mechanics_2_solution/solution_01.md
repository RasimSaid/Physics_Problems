1. Gravitational DependenceProblem: A simple pendulum has a period of 4s on Earth. What is its period on the Moon ($g_m = \frac{1}{6}g_e$)? Also, find the length $L$ for $T = 1\text{s}$ on Earth.Solution:The formula for the period is $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$.On the Moon: Since $T \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$, if gravity is $1/6$, the period increases by $\sqrt{6}$.$$T_{moon} = T_{earth} \times \sqrt{6} = 4 \times 2.45 \approx \mathbf{9.80\text{ s}}$$Length on Earth:$$1 = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{9.81}} \implies L = \frac{9.81}{4\pi^2} \approx \mathbf{0.248\text{ m (or 24.8 cm)}}$$
